01 / RESEARCH AT FLORIDA TECH

So, what do I actually do in a cancer research lab?

At Florida Tech's MOSAIC Lab, I work with cancer data and try to find patterns that might actually be useful. It involves a lot of coding, a lot of statistics, and occasionally reminding my computer that we're on the same team.

✦
Medical Imaging / Radiomics

Lung Cancer Radiomics

CT scans aren't just pictures. We can turn them into numerical features describing things like texture, shape, and patterns inside lung nodules. I study how those measurements relate to radiologists' assessments, including how much those assessments can differ between doctors.

One big question is whether the numbers we extract are consistent enough to be useful. It's a surprisingly interesting mix of medical imaging, statistics, and figuring out why four people can look at the same thing and disagree.

Python · R · CT Imaging · Feature Analysis
🧬
Genomics / Survival Analysis

Genomic Survival Analysis

For this project, I work with gene-expression and clinical data from lung cancer patients to explore whether genetic information can help us understand survival outcomes.

I use statistical survival models, including Cox regression, and compare models built from clinical information, genomic information, and both together. With thousands of genes and far fewer patients, choosing useful signals without overfitting is a pretty big part of the challenge.

R · Cox Regression · Genomics · Model Validation

These are ongoing research projects, so I'm sharing what I'm working on rather than posting research datasets or unfinished results.

03 / THE QUESTION I GET A LOT

Why applied math?

Because I like knowing what I can actually do with the math.

Whenever I tell someone I'm a math major, I think they assume I spend all day solving equations for fun. And while I've definitely done my fair share of proofs, that's not really why I chose it.

Pure mathematics often focuses on understanding mathematical ideas themselves: why things work, how to prove them, and what follows from them. Applied mathematics takes those tools and asks what we can do with them in the real world. There's overlap, of course, and both matter.

That's the part I love. One day I can use statistics to study cancer data, the next I can work on investment risk, and after that I can explore patterns in marketplace data. Completely different problems, but the same way of thinking.

I'm studying Applied Mathematics with a concentration in Artificial Intelligence and Data Science, plus minors in Finance and English Literature. The English minor might seem like a plot twist, but I like that my interests don't all fit in one box.
